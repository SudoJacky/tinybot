## Writing employee assignments

Describe the employee's role, responsibilities and boundaries in member
instructions. Put the concrete work and completion criteria in each task's
instructions, since one employee may receive several tasks. Each assignment must
stand on its own without the parent conversation.

Use the user's language and organize task instructions around these points:

- **Outcome:** the decision, questions or change this task must address, and who
  will use its result.
- **Scope:** the relevant subject, sources or files and the intended depth.
  Identify adjacent work owned by other employees or outside the request.
- **Deliverable:** the expected content or format, owned output paths when files
  are needed, and the dependency results to reuse.
- **Evidence and checks:** how important claims or changes should be verified.
  Use sources and as-of dates where relevant; for implementation work, specify
  observable behavior and appropriate validation.
- **Completion criteria:** concrete conditions the employee can check before
  handing off and the coordinator can assess afterwards. Tie each condition to
  the requested outcome. "Research thoroughly" or "write a good report" is not
  a completion criterion.
- **Stopping and gaps:** stop once those conditions are met. Additional work
  should resolve a specific remaining question or conflict within scope. If a
  required condition cannot be met, explain the attempted checks, missing
  evidence and effect on the conclusion in unresolved; do not claim it was met.

Keep the scope proportionate to the user's request. A general overview should
cover its core questions; exhaustive inventories and repeated verification need
a reason tied to the requested outcome. Respect explicit user depth, budget and
coverage requirements. Do not invent word counts, source quotas or tool-call
limits as a substitute for completion criteria. Summary text has no length cap.

For example, an overview of a project's release could be complete when its
purpose, officially released assets and documented usage are explained with
sources, each requested comparison is addressed, and contradictions or unavailable
facts are identified. Cataloging every community derivative would be a separate
scope choice, not an automatic extension of that overview.

These points belong inside the existing instructions strings; they do not add
JSON fields. Adapt their detail to the task. When reviewing a handoff, compare it
with the assignment's criteria and unresolved issues: successful publication
records delivery, not proof that every criterion or factual claim was verified.
